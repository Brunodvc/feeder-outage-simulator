This models the full path of electricity: a power plant feeds a step-up substation, which raises the plant's 13 kV output to 500 kV for long-distance transmission lines. A step-down substation lowers it to 35 kV for the primary distribution feeder, and distribution transformers near the customers drop it to the 120 V secondary service that runs to each home. Faulting any line or stopping a plant cascades an outage to everything downstream of it; closing a normally-open tie switch can restore isolated customers from a different, still-powered path, the same strategy utilities use to minimize outage scope during a real fault.
